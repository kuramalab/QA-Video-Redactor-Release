**Covers that hold, fewer false alarms**

- The small fields of a payment form - the security code, the expiry month
  and year - stayed readable under too faint a veil: the blur held back so
  as not to swell over a keyboard, and on a short box it was not enough. A
  short box is now blurred in full.
- The keyboard cover no longer climbs into the page: it measures where the
  bed of the keys ends and stops there, with one key of margin for the
  keycap that pops up enlarged - on a full keyboard and on a numeric pad
  alike.
- A form field behind a label - "Name", "City", "Postcode", "Province" and
  their kin, in six languages - is covered for what it holds, whatever is
  typed in it; a sentence that merely names those words is not.
- A personal detail no longer surfaces during a fast scroll or a fade: the
  cover travels with the page and stays while the text beneath is still
  discernible, then lets go. A postcode glued to its town and misread
  ("881ooCatanzaro") is recognised and repaired.
- Payment windows are opened only by the real card fields: the prices and
  page numbers of a shopping cart are no longer treated as card digits.
- A number that does not pass a card's or a VAT's own check is no longer
  learned as a secret: a listing id inside a URL, or a number on a phone's
  "package info" screen, no longer make patches of page get covered
  elsewhere.
